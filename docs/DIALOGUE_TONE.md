# Dialogue tone and humor

Keep the world ominous, but let people be funny inside it. Use original dry jokes, practical complaints, small rivalries and occasional gallows humor. Characters should sound like residents coping with this world, not commentators on a game. Build callbacks sparingly; no copied catchphrase is required.

First implemented voices:

- Edda: blunt smithing advice, inconvenient undead and aging knees that forecast weather. Lines vary across quest stages.
- Hadrik: practical soldier's humor. A shield is a portable door; stay on the indoor side.
- Sera: dry jabs at mundane failures and Hadrik's distrust of magic.
- Runa: frustration with committees and disappearing supplies.
- Mereth: archival deadpan, including a screaming safety manual filed under Fiction.
- Aelin: earthy, slightly threatening humor about returning things to nature.
- Rook: station safety and staffing jokes after a security disaster.
- Ilyra: understated expedition survival and the expense of a return beacon.

Quest instructions and rewards remain clear. Give humor space between serious revelations. The missing-family record texts remain serious, and Mereth's safety-manual joke is suppressed once that investigation begins. Future passes can add optional conversational branches and occasional recurring callbacks without making every NPC a comedian. Current implementation is written dialogue; no voiced banter or random ambient bark scheduler has been added.

## Merchant voices

All five shopkeepers now have four original greetings each: two everyday lines and two unlocked by completed chapters. Torren jokes about carrying loot, Mira about practical medicine, Halvar about food and inconvenient customers, Brenna about dangerous workmanship, and mountain trader Oren about tolls and invoices. Milestone lines enter the greeting rotation only after their named story flag is complete. Repeat visits rotate eligible lines during the current session; relaunch resets the rotation.

Greetings appear when a shop opens. Transaction results and errors replace them immediately. They do not change prices, stock or rewards. This is written shop dialogue, not ambient or voiced banter.

## Tovin at Mirewatch

Lights Beneath the Boards introduces Tovin, a stranded scavenger whose jokes cover his embarrassment. His lock inspection was “Very thorough. Terrible business model.” After rescue he intends to check tombs' return policies; his reward was formerly his funeral fund. The sequence makes the signal, winch and cache objectives explicit, with the humor following the practical information.

## Veyra's creditor

The Astronomer's Debt gives Veyra dry financial humor alongside a concrete choice: her creditor threatens to repossess the stars, while her weather forecasts and repayment promises earn equal skepticism. Neither outcome portrays the player as having failed the quest. Debt settlement and public research each receive a distinct closing line; the latter explicitly leaves repayment to Veyra's work.

## The final watch

Letters Above the Snow gives Hadrik and Sera conflicting family memories, then lets the memorial record establish what happened. Keep their grief sincere. Runa's remark that grief refuses to observe the agenda targets her own administrative habits, not the dead. Public remembrance and private replies both acknowledge the same truth without presenting either family as foolish.

## Iven's field research

The Apprentice Who Vanished uses self-directed humor: Mereth prioritizes the apprentice over replaceable paperwork, while Iven describes waiting without lunch as a treatment for arrogance. His final choices preserve authorship in both paths and distinguish supervised study from independent work. The dialogue presents fictional resin research without adding a real medical claim or treatment mechanic.
